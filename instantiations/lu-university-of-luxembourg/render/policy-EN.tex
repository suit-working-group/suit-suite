\section*{The three foundational ideas of the Technical Reference}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{The three foundational ideas of the Technical Reference}

\subsection*{1. The sovereignty grid}
The Technical Reference's normative pivot is a five-dimension grid that
operationalises sovereignty in university \gls{it}. The five dimensions are
\keyword{Data sovereignty}, \keyword{Technical sovereignty}, \keyword{Financial
sovereignty}, \keyword{Operational sovereignty}, and \keyword{Institutional
sovereignty}. Each dimension is independently scored on a four-point scale
(Absent, Partial, Established, Robust); no aggregation is permitted; the grid is
a deliberation instrument, not an audit scoring system.

\subsection*{2. The reference architecture on open foundations}
The Technical Reference develops a vendor-neutral architecture organised in nine
functional layers grouped in three planes: a \keyword{Subject and Trust plane}
(Identity, Endpoint, Cryptographic services), an \keyword{Execution plane}
(Network, Compute, Storage), and a \keyword{Control and Governance plane}
(Policy, Observability, Orchestration). The architecture's normative content is
the set of interface contracts (open standards) that connect the layers; a
reference instantiation on open foundations demonstrates realisability,
proprietary alternatives that respect the contracts remain admissible.

\subsection*{3. The migration playbook for mixed estates}
The Technical Reference proposes a wave-based migration playbook from any
brownfield position toward the target architecture. Ten waves (Wave~0 to Wave~9)
unfold under a documented dependency graph; each declares its goal,
pre-requisites, coexistence pattern, trajectories, reversibility criteria, and
exit criteria gated by a structured conformance suite. Reversibility is
non-negotiable: every wave is reversible at acceptable cost during a published
window.
